TLN Cybersecurity Challenge 2026
Technology is evolving quickly, and so are the threats that come with it.

The TLN Cybersecurity Challenge 2026 is a hackathon hosted by Tech Literacy Network where participants will use cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and technology to build solutions to real-world problems.

Whether you're interested in cybersecurity, AI, software development, privacy, or problem-solving, this challenge is an opportunity to turn an idea into a working project.

The Challenge
Build a technology-based solution that addresses a problem related to cybersecurity, online safety, or digital security.

You can use AI as a core part of your project, use it to enhance an existing cybersecurity solution, or build a solution that addresses an emerging threat created by AI.

Some areas you could explore include:

AI-powered cybersecurity
Phishing and scam detection
Online privacy and data protection
Cyberbullying and online safety
Any other cybersecurity-related problem you want to tackle
You don't need to build the next major cybersecurity platform. We care more about the problem you're solving, how you approach it, and the impact your solution could have.

Why Participate?
You'll have the opportunity to:

Build a project from an idea
Explore AI and cybersecurity
Work with other students and developers
Showcase your project
Receive feedback from judges
Projects will be evaluated based on their idea, technical implementation, creativity, cybersecurity relevance, and potential impact.

Requirements
Submission Requirements
Each team or individual must submit the following:

1. Build a Cybersecurity Solution
Create a working prototype or demonstration that addresses a clear, real-world problem related to:

Cybersecurity
Online safety
Privacy and data protection
Digital security
Your project should demonstrate a meaningful attempt to solve the problem, whether through a web application, mobile application, software tool, prototype, or other technology-based solution.

2. Devpost Project Submission
Your Devpost submission must include:

Project Name - The name of your project.
Project Description - Briefly explain what you built and why.
Technologies Used - List the programming languages, frameworks, APIs, tools, and other technologies used.
3. Project Demo
Where possible, provide a link that allows judges to explore or test your project.

This could be:

Live website
Working application
GitHub repository
Prototype
If your project cannot be hosted or made publicly accessible, you can demonstrate it through your submission video instead.

4. Demo Video
Submit a video under 5 minutes that demonstrates your project and clearly explains:

The Problem - What cybersecurity issue are you addressing?
Your Solution - What did you build?
How It Works - Demonstrate the key features.
Technology - What technologies did you use?
Impact - Who could benefit from your solution and how?
5. AI & External Tools Disclosure
AI tools and other external resources are allowed during the hackathon. However, participants must disclose any significant use of AI tools in their project.

Briefly explain:

Which AI tools were used
How they were used
Participants are responsible for understanding their submitted work and should be able to explain their code, design decisions, and overall solution.

Rules TLN Cybersecurity Challenge 2026 - Rules
 

Original work: Projects should be created primarily during the hackathon. Participants may use existing open-source libraries, frameworks, APIs, datasets, and other publicly available resources.

Team size: Participants may work individually or in teams of up to 4 people.

Cybersecurity: Projects should have a meaningful connection to cybersecurity, online safety, privacy, and digital security.

Responsible security research: Projects must not intentionally damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to systems, networks, accounts, or data.

No malicious software: Submissions must not contain malware, ransomware, credential stealers, spyware, or other software intended to cause harm or obtain unauthorized access. 

AI usage: AI tools are permitted. Participants must disclose significant use of AI tools and remain responsible for understanding and presenting their submitted work.

Intellectual property: Participants must have the right to use all code, datasets, images, APIs, and other resources included in their submission.

Respect: Participants must treat other participants, organizers, mentors, judges, and volunteers respectfully.

Judging: Projects will be evaluated based on criteria such as:

Technical implementation
Creativity and innovation
Cybersecurity relevance
Practicality and potential impact
Quality of the demonstration
Overall project execution
Disqualification: Organizers reserve the right to disqualify submissions that violate these rules, applicable laws, or the spirit of the challenge.

Code of Conduct: Participants are expected to maintain a respectful, inclusive, and professional environment throughout the event.

Project Advice
Start with the problem, not the technology.

A strong submission should be able to answer three questions:

What problem are you solving?
Why does it matter?
Why is your solution a good way to solve it?
